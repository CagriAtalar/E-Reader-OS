A Brief History of Operating Systems
=====================================

An operating system (OS) is the software that manages computer hardware and
provides services for application programs. The history of operating systems
is the history of computing itself.

1950s: Batch Processing
------------------------

The earliest computers had no operating system at all. Programs were loaded
one at a time using punch cards, and the computer would execute them
sequentially. This was known as batch processing.

Operators would physically load program cards into the machine, wait for the
program to complete, and then load the next one. This was slow and wasteful,
as the expensive computer hardware sat idle between jobs.

1960s: Time-Sharing
--------------------

The concept of time-sharing emerged in the 1960s. Multiple users could share
a single computer by rapidly switching between their programs. Each user
appeared to have the machine to themselves.

CTSS (Compatible Time-Sharing System) at MIT was one of the first time-sharing
systems. It led to the development of Multics, which in turn inspired Unix.

1970s: Unix
-----------

Unix was created by Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie at Bell Labs in 1969. It
was written in C, making it portable across different hardware platforms. This
was revolutionary -- previous operating systems were tied to specific machines.

Unix introduced many concepts still used today: hierarchical file systems,
pipes, shell scripting, and the philosophy of small, composable tools.

1980s: Personal Computers
--------------------------

The IBM PC, introduced in 1981, brought computing to the masses. MS-DOS
became the dominant operating system for personal computers, followed by
early versions of Windows.

Apple introduced the Macintosh in 1984 with a graphical user interface,
inspired by work done at Xerox PARC. This made computers accessible to
non-technical users for the first time.

1990s: The Internet Age
------------------------

The 1990s saw the rise of Windows 95, which combined the ease of use of a
graphical interface with the power of a 32-bit operating system. Linux,
created by Linus Torvalds in 1991, began as a hobby project and grew into
a major force in server computing.

The World Wide Web, invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989, transformed how
people used computers and drove demand for more capable operating systems.

2000s and Beyond
-----------------

Modern operating systems are incredibly complex pieces of software. They
manage multiple processors, gigabytes of memory, and sophisticated security
models. Mobile operating systems like Android and iOS have brought computing
to billions of people worldwide.

Yet the fundamental challenges remain the same: managing hardware resources
efficiently, providing a stable platform for applications, and keeping users
safe from malicious software.
